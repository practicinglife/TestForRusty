## 1. Blacksmithing Workflow (Beginning to End)

### 1.1 Raw Material Sourcing

#### Steel Selection
- **Carbon Steel (1045, 1075, 1095)**: Ideal for knives and tools
- **Mild Steel (A36, 1018)**: Best for decorative work and hardware
- **Tool Steel (O1, W2)**: Premium knife-making steel
- **Wrought Iron**: Authentic look for traditional pieces

#### Sourcing Options
- Local steel suppliers and metal service centers
- Online suppliers (Admiral Steel, New Jersey Steel Baron, Jantz Supply)
- Salvage yards for recycled materials
- Farm equipment dealers for leaf springs and files

### 1.2 Shop Setup and Forge Preparation

#### Daily Setup Checklist
1. Inspect all equipment for damage or wear
2. Check fuel supply (propane, coal, or charcoal)
3. Verify ventilation systems are operational
4. Organize tools at workstations
5. Review project plans and gather required materials
6. Don appropriate safety gear

#### Forge Types and Setup
| Forge Type | Fuel | Best For | Setup Time |
|------------|------|----------|------------|
| Gas Forge | Propane | Consistent heat, beginners | 10-15 min |
| Coal Forge | Coal/Coke | Traditional work, welding | 20-30 min |
| Charcoal Forge | Charcoal | Budget-friendly, small items | 15-20 min |

### 1.3 Safety Procedures

#### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
- **Eye Protection**: Safety glasses with side shields (ANSI Z87.1 rated)
- **Hearing Protection**: Earplugs or muffs (NRR 25+)
- **Respiratory Protection**: N95 masks minimum; P100 for grinding
- **Hand Protection**: Leather gloves for handling; bare hands near forge
- **Body Protection**: Leather apron, long sleeves (cotton/wool, no synthetics)
- **Foot Protection**: Steel-toe leather boots

#### Fire Safety
- Keep fire extinguisher (ABC rated) within 10 feet of forge
- Maintain clear paths to exits
- Store flammables away from heat sources
- Have water bucket nearby for quenching and emergencies
- Install smoke and carbon monoxide detectors

#### Ventilation Requirements
- Minimum 200 CFM exhaust for gas forges
- 500+ CFM for coal forges
- Side draft hoods positioned 12-18 inches above fire pot
- Fresh air intake on opposite side of shop

### 1.4 Heating Cycles and Temperature Guide

#### Color Temperature Reference
| Color | Temperature (°F) | Best For |
|-------|------------------|----------|
| Black Heat | Below 900°F | No forging |
| Faint Red | 900-1000°F | Light bending only |
| Dark Red | 1050-1175°F | Heavy bending, punching |
| Cherry Red | 1175-1300°F | General forging |
| Bright Cherry | 1300-1400°F | Drawing out, upsetting |
| Orange | 1400-1500°F | Forge welding prep |
| Light Orange | 1500-1650°F | Forge welding |
| Yellow | 1650-1800°F | Forge welding (high carbon) |
| Light Yellow | 1800-2000°F | Burning range - AVOID |

#### Heat Treatment Cycles

**For High Carbon Steel (1095):**
1. **Normalizing**: Heat to 1500°F, air cool (repeat 3x)
2. **Annealing**: Heat to 1475°F, cool slowly in vermiculite
3. **Hardening**: Heat to 1475°F, quench in appropriate oil
4. **Tempering**: Heat to 400-450°F, hold 2 hours, air cool

### 1.5 Core Hammering Techniques

#### Basic Techniques
- **Drawing Out**: Lengthening and thinning metal
- **Upsetting**: Thickening metal by compressing
- **Bending**: Creating angles and curves
- **Punching**: Creating holes through material
- **Drifting**: Enlarging and shaping holes
- **Splitting**: Dividing material with chisel
- **Scrolling**: Creating decorative spirals
- **Twisting**: Rotating heated bar on its axis

#### Hammer Control
- Let the hammer do the work; use controlled drops
- Strike flat with hammer face
- Work in heats of 30-60 seconds
- Rotate workpiece for even working
- Plan hammer blows before striking

### 1.6 Quenching and Tempering

#### Quenchants by Steel Type
| Steel | Quenchant | Temperature |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1095 | Fast oil (Parks #50) | 120-140°F |
| 1084 | Canola oil | 100-130°F |
| O1 | Oil | 120-140°F |
| W2 | Water or brine | 60-80°F |
| 5160 | Oil | 120-140°F |

#### Tempering Guidelines
- Always temper immediately after quenching
- Use calibrated oven for consistent results
- Typical knife tempering: 375-425°F
- Tool tempering: 450-500°F
- Spring steel: 500-600°F

### 1.7 Grinding, Polishing, and Finishing

#### Grinding Progression
1. **Rough Grinding (36-60 grit)**: Shape profile, establish bevels
2. **Shaping (80-120 grit)**: Refine bevels, remove deep scratches
3. **Finishing (220-400 grit)**: Prepare for heat treatment
4. **Post-HT Grinding (400-800 grit)**: Final shaping
5. **Polishing (1000-2500 grit)**: Mirror finish (optional)

#### Finishing Options
- **Forge Scale**: Leave natural oxide layer for rustic look
- **Wire Brushing**: Clean finish with texture
- **Sanding/Polishing**: Smooth, refined appearance
- **Patina/Forced Oxidation**: Controlled surface coloring
- **Clear Coat**: Protection for decorative pieces
- **Wax**: Food-safe protection for culinary items
- **Oil Finish**: Traditional protection for tools

### 1.8 Workflow Variations by Product Type

#### Knives
1. Design and template creation
2. Steel selection and cutting
3. Profile grinding
4. Bevel grinding
5. Normalizing (3 cycles)
6. Thermal cycling
7. Hardening quench
8. Tempering (2 cycles)
9. Final grinding and sharpening
10. Handle fitting and finishing
11. Sheath making (optional)

#### Tools (Hammers, Tongs, Chisels)
1. Stock selection (appropriate for tool type)
2. Drawing out and shaping
3. Punching eye/socket (if applicable)
4. Handle fitting/welding
5. Heat treatment (if required)
6. Surface finishing
7. Handle installation

#### Decorative Ironwork
1. Design and full-scale drawing
2. Material calculation and cutting
3. Individual element forging
4. Assembly and joinery (rivets, collars, welds)
5. Surface finishing
6. Protective coating application
7. Installation hardware preparation

#### Hardware (Hinges, Latches, Hooks)
1. Template creation for consistency
2. Batch production setup
3. Forging with jigs for repeatability
4. Punching/drilling mounting holes
5. Surface finishing
6. Coating for corrosion resistance

#### Layout Principles
- Forge, anvil, and slack tub form primary triangle
- Tools within arm's reach of anvil
- Clear traffic patterns
- Grinding isolated for dust control
- Raw materials near cutting station
- Finished goods near shipping area

## 4. Company Structure

### 4.1 Recommended Legal Structures

#### Sole Proprietorship
**Best For**: Beginners, part-time operations, testing the market

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Simplest to set up | Personal liability for business debts |
| Lowest cost to establish | Harder to raise capital |
| Complete control | Business ends with owner |
| Pass-through taxation | May appear less professional |
| Minimal paperwork | Personal assets at risk |

**Setup Requirements**:
- Business license (city/county)
- DBA ("Doing Business As") registration
- EIN (optional but recommended)
- Business bank account
- **Cost**: $50-$200

#### Limited Liability Company (LLC)
**Best For**: Serious operations, those wanting liability protection

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Personal asset protection | More paperwork |
| Professional appearance | Annual fees in most states |
| Flexible taxation options | Operating agreement needed |
| Easier to add partners | Slightly more complex |
| Pass-through taxation | State-specific regulations |

**Setup Requirements**:
- Articles of Organization
- Operating Agreement
- EIN
- Business licenses
- Registered agent
- **Cost**: $100-$500 (varies by state)

#### S Corporation
**Best For**: Established businesses with significant profits

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Potential tax savings | More complex setup |
| Professional credibility | Strict requirements |
| Liability protection | Required meetings/records |
| Salary/distribution flexibility | Additional tax filings |

**Recommendation**: Start as Sole Proprietorship or LLC, transition to S-Corp when annual profits exceed $40,000+

| **Drawing out** | Lengthening steel by hammering |
| **Fuller** | Tool or process for creating grooves |
| **Hardening** | Heat treatment to make steel hard |
| **Heat** | Single warming of steel for working |
| **Normalizing** | Heat treatment to relieve stress |
| **Quenching** | Rapid cooling to harden steel |
| **Scale** | Oxide layer formed during heating |
| **Slack tub** | Container of water for cooling work |
| **Tempering** | Reheating hardened steel to reduce brittleness |
| **Upsetting** | Thickening steel by compressing |
